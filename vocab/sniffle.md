# Flash Card: Sniffle

## Back

<div id="back">
  <strong>Translation</strong>: "I've been sniffling all morning, I must have caught a cold." / "He was sniffling throughout the whole film because of his cold."<br /><br />
  <strong>Word</strong>: sniffle (verb)<br /><br />
  <strong>Explanation</strong>: to keep sniffing repeatedly through the nose, usually because of a cold or crying<br /><br />
  <strong>Examples</strong>:<br />
  She sniffled quietly during the sad movie.<br />
  He's been sniffling all week with a cold.<br />
  The toddler sniffled after scraping his knee.<br /><br />
  <strong>Have the sniffles vs. have a runny nose</strong>: "Have the sniffles" describes having a mild cold overall, focusing on the repeated sniffing sound/action it causes (e.g. "I've got the sniffles today"). "Have a runny nose" describes just the specific symptom — nasal discharge or congestion — without implying the sniffing sound or a full cold (e.g. "I have a runny nose from the pollen, but otherwise I feel fine"). In short: sniffles is a general, sound-focused way of saying you're a bit sick; runny nose is a precise, symptom-only description that can happen even without being ill.
</div>
